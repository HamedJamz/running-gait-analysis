# Validation results

Generated by `scripts/validate.py` from all 28 participants x 3 speeds.

## 1. Sagittal joint angles vs. Visual3D (published processed curves)

Median over 168 curves per joint (28 participants x 3 speeds x 2 sides).

| Joint | Pearson r | RMSE (deg) | Mean offset (deg) | RMSE after removing offset (deg) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hip flexion | 0.991 | 2.8 | +0.8 | 2.6 |
| Knee flexion | 0.987 | 5.5 | -1.7 | 5.1 |
| Ankle dorsiflexion | 0.969 | 6.2 | +4.7 | 3.6 |

Waveform shapes agree closely (high r). Remaining offsets come from different segment definitions (e.g. hip joint centre regression, foot axis and neutral reference), which shift a curve without changing its shape.

## 2. Marker-only gait events vs. force plate

7168 matched contacts. Difference = kinematic − force plate.

| Event | Bias (ms) | SD (ms) | 95% limits of agreement (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial contact | -7.4 | 9.8 | -26.6 to +11.8 |
| Toe-off | +0.2 | 7.3 | -14.0 to +14.5 |

For reference, one marker frame is 6.7 ms. The marker-only method can be used when no force plate is available (e.g. overground or field testing).

## 3. Peak vertical ground reaction force vs. published processed data

Mean difference +0.031 BW (SD 0.027), r = 0.994, n = 168 (subject x speed x side).

Force plausibility check (mean vertical force over the whole trial / body weight; ideal = 1): median 0.978, range 0.933–1.003. All trials within ±7 %. The small, consistent shortfall (~2–3 %) is compatible with body mass having been measured with clothing/shoes or with a small plate offset; it is reported, not corrected.

## 4. Foot-strike pattern vs. dataset metadata

Rows: label in the dataset metadata; columns: this pipeline (foot-strike angle, Altman & Davis 2012 thresholds). Unit = subject x speed x side.

| metadata \ pipeline | Rearfoot | Midfoot | Forefoot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rearfoot | 106 | 8 | 0 |
| Midfoot | 4 | 20 | 3 |
| Forefoot | 0 | 19 | 8 |

Exact agreement 80 %; rearfoot vs. non-rearfoot agreement 93 %. The metadata labels come from the original authors' own classification, not from the foot-strike angle, so perfect agreement is not expected; the disagreements are almost all at the midfoot/forefoot boundary.
